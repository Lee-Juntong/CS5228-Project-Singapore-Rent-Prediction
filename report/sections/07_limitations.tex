\section{Limitations and Future Work}\label{sec:limitations}

The data do not record the floor of the unit, its condition or actual furnishing, or whether the whole flat or only some rooms are rented. These attributes probably explain part of the rent, and no model in this report can recover that part.

Rents were nearly flat during our six-month holdout, while the test period extends up to 16 months beyond training. We therefore compared raw and index targets at five earlier training cutoffs (Table~\ref{tab:horizon}, Appendix~\ref{app:backtest}). For forecasts 7--16 months ahead, the index reduced LightGBM's RMSE from 909 to 762~SGD at the March 2022 cutoff, when rents continued to rise. At the March 2023 cutoff, it raised RMSE from 569 to 955~SGD because the fitted trend continued upward after rents levelled off. By the March 2024 cutoff, the fitted trend had slowed to 9.8~SGD per month, and LightGBM scored 451~SGD with the index target versus 455~SGD with the raw target over months 7--12, the remaining months with observed rents. The index refitted on all training rows for the final submission has a slope of 5.3~SGD per month, which points to the low risk case. Among the seven baselines, the group median benefited from the index at all five cutoffs. Giving each cutoff equal weight (Table~\ref{tab:baseline-horizon}), its average longer-horizon RMSE fell from 998.7 to 700.6~SGD. For KNN, the same average rose from 655.7 to 697.3~SGD.

Unit storey and whether the tenancy covers a whole flat or a room could help explain the spread in rents among otherwise similar records. We would also evaluate the blend on an earlier window that does not overlap the holdout and tune the models that use rent lags.

We also tested a rent index that gave more weight to recent monthly medians and adjusted them using the GDP growth forecasts of the Ministry of Trade and Industry~\cite{mti_gdp_forecasts} and the headline inflation forecasts of the Monetary Authority of Singapore~\cite{mas_mps} available at the forecast date. Over the six months after the September 2024 cutoff, adding these forecasts reduced monthly median RMSE from 52.3 to 45.5~SGD, compared with 43.1~SGD for the original linear index (Table~\ref{tab:macro-medians}). We therefore retained the linear index. Future work could allow the estimated rent trend to flatten when recent growth slows or test indicators more closely linked to rental demand and supply, such as work pass counts and housing completions.
