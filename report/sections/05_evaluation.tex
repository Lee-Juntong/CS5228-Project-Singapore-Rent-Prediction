The simple models get closer than we expected. With the index target, the group median lookup is 37~SGD behind tuned LightGBM and Ridge is 22~SGD behind, against 305 and 126~SGD on raw rent. Linear models and KNN use restricted inputs (34 and 4 columns), so these gaps do not isolate the algorithm alone. Boosting adds little over a forest on this data. On raw rent tuned boosting beats the forest by 9 to 10~SGD, and with the index target the untuned forest beats tuned boosting by 1 to 5~SGD. Tuning gains 4 to 7~SGD on raw rent. The top three models lie within 1.5~SGD of each other, and tuned XGBoost and LightGBM are 0.05~SGD apart on the public leaderboard, so we treat them as a tie.

\subsection{Potential Outlier Points}\label{sec:suspicious}

The EDA flagged rows whose rent is arguably an outlier when put in correlation with other attributes, using the two rules in Table~\ref{tab:outliers}, and the decision was to run feature ablation against said rows as well.

We retrained tuned LightGBM without flagged rows in the fit period, on the index target. Dropping the 49 rows with a low rent for their area changes the holdout RMSE by $-0.1$~SGD and the public score by $-0.4$~SGD. Dropping the 12 rows flagged by both of our rules adds 0.4~SGD. Dropping the 331 rows that are extreme within their flat type or town adds 2.2~SGD on the holdout and 4.2~SGD on the public leaderboard, probably because that rule also removes real premium rents. We keep all rows.

\subsection{Final Model}

Table~\ref{tab:blend} compares the blends on the holdout, the 16 month window and the public leaderboard. The repeat-rent features gain on all three: the five model blend beats tuned LightGBM by 14.8~SGD on the holdout, 18.3~SGD on the 16 month window and 19.4~SGD on the public score. On the 16 month window the index models over-predict by about 120~SGD, because the slope fitted in late 2023 was too steep. The mix of index and raw targets cuts this to 87~SGD and has the best 16 month score, but its public score is 4.6~SGD worse.

The five model blend is our final Kaggle submission, because it has the best public score and the best holdout score. We chose it on the same windows that it is scored on, and the public score was part of that choice, so all three of its scores are slightly optimistic.
