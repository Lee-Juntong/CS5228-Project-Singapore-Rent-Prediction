\section{Evaluation}\label{sec:evaluation}

\subsection{Model Comparison}

Table~\ref{tab:results} compares models on the six-month holdout using the index target. Table~\ref{tab:trend} gives the corresponding raw-target results for the single models. On the same holdout with the index target, adding rental history (Table~\ref{tab:baseline-lags}) reduced RMSE from 467.9 to 449.0~SGD for Ridge, from 481.9 to 451.2~SGD for KNN, and from 444.3 to 440.2~SGD for the random forest. For the single decision tree, RMSE rose from 473.3 to 492.0~SGD.

The simple models get closer than we expected. With the index target, the group median lookup is 37~SGD behind tuned LightGBM and Ridge is 22~SGD behind, against 305 and 126~SGD on raw rent. Boosting adds little over a forest on these data. On raw rent tuned boosting beats the forest by 9 to 10~SGD, and with the index target the untuned forest beats tuned boosting by 1 to 5~SGD. Tuning gains 4 to 7~SGD on raw rent. The untuned random forest (444.3~SGD), tuned XGBoost (445.6~SGD) and tuned LightGBM (445.8~SGD) lie within 1.5~SGD of each other, and tuned XGBoost and LightGBM are 0.05~SGD apart on the public leaderboard, so we treat them as a tie.

\subsection{Feature Group Ablation}

Adding location features reduced raw-target RMSE by 10 to 14~SGD for the two boosting models in Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and by 10.1~SGD for the random forest. KNN gained the most from location: adding latitude and longitude reduced its RMSE from 545.5 to 482.3~SGD. The effect of market indicators depended on the model. Adding them raised RMSE by 2.6~SGD for LightGBM and 1.6~SGD for CatBoost, but lowered it from 575.8 to 479.5~SGD for Ridge. These indicators describe the approval month and would not be known at forecast time, so we excluded them from the final model.

We retrained tuned LightGBM on the index target without the flagged rows in the fit period, with features and hyperparameters fixed. The thresholds come from the fit period only, so holdout rents do not affect which rows are dropped. Dropping the 36 rows with a low rent for their area adds 0.5~SGD to the holdout RMSE of 445.8~SGD. Dropping the 9 rows flagged by both rules adds 0.4~SGD. Dropping the 374 rows that are extreme within their flat type or town adds 3.3~SGD, probably because that rule also removes real premium rents. On the public leaderboard, where the model trains on all labelled rows, dropping the 49 low rent rows changes the score by $-0.4$~SGD and dropping the 331 extreme rows adds 4.2~SGD. We keep all rows.

\subsection{Final Model}

Table~\ref{tab:blend} compares the blends on the holdout, the 16 month window and the public leaderboard. The two model blend is tuned LightGBM plus the regularised LightGBM, and the index and raw mix fits these two models on both targets. The repeat-rent features and the blend gain on all three: the five model blend beats tuned LightGBM by 14.8~SGD on the holdout, 18.3~SGD on the 16 month window and 19.4~SGD on the public score. The regularised LightGBM alone gains 9.9 and 13.2~SGD on the first two. On the 16 month window the index models over-predict by about 120~SGD, because the slope fitted in late 2023 was too steep. The mix of index and raw targets cuts this to 87~SGD and has the best 16 month score, but its public score is 4.6~SGD worse.
