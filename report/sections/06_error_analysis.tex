\section{Error Analysis and Interpretation}\label{sec:errors}

Sections~\ref{sec:residuals} and~\ref{sec:interpretation} analyse tuned LightGBM with the index target (holdout RMSE 445.8, MAE 323.9, bias $-24.5$~SGD), the base member of the blend. Residual means prediction minus observed rent.

\subsection{Why the RMSE Is Not Lower}\label{sec:floor}

Rows with the same block, flat type and month have identical inputs, so any model gives them one prediction, and the spread of rent inside such a group is error that no model can remove. Even with the true mean of every group, the RMSE would stay at 178~SGD on all training rows, 165~SGD on the holdout and 187~SGD on the 16 month window. This bound is far too low to reach, because it counts the 74\% of rows with unique inputs as predicted perfectly.

The spread inside the groups is a better guide. Rows that share inputs have a pooled standard deviation of SGD 449 on the holdout and SGD 468 on all training rows. On 4{,}207 repeated holdout rows, the mean rent of the other rows in the group scores 612~SGD against 446~SGD for the model, so even the rent of a near identical flat is a noisy guide. If rows with unique inputs have a similar spread, an RMSE of 431~SGD is close to what these inputs allow. We cannot prove this floor, and the repeat-rent features showed that earlier estimates of it were too high.

Leaderboard scores of about 160 to 180 sit at this bound. We infer that those teams look up test rents in the public HDB rental table on data.gov.sg, which records month, town, block, street, flat type and rent and covers the test period. A lookup returns the rents of a group, so it can reach the bound but not pass it. From the given inputs alone, a score of 200 would need rows with unique inputs predicted within about 107~SGD, while rents in one group differ by about 450~SGD. Our models use no rents from this table.

\subsection{Residuals}\label{sec:residuals}

A few large errors carry much of the squared error. Absolute errors above SGD 1{,}000 occur in 3.9\% of holdout rows and give 38\% of the squared error (Fig.~\ref{fig:outliers}). The 585 rows with a rent below SGD 2{,}000 are 3.4\% of the rows and give 26\% of the squared error, with a bias of $+1{,}112$~SGD. The 20 largest errors are all over-predictions, and nine of them have a rent below SGD 1{,}000. In two pairs of rows the recorded profile is identical while the rents are SGD 3{,}550 and 800, and SGD 2{,}880 and 700.

\centerline{\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{validation_outliers}}
\caption{Predicted against observed rent on the holdout (left) and the share of rows and squared error by rent band (right).}
\label{fig:outliers}

The data cannot explain these rows. They may be a room or one tenant's share of the rent, while area and type describe the whole flat. They may also be discounted agreements or errors in the declared amount. One SGD 650 row matches the public HDB rental table, so the amount was published. HDB states that owners declare these rents and that it does not verify them, so the match says nothing about the cause.

Large flats and central areas do not carry most of the error. The 5-room and executive flats are 28\% of the rows and 31\% of the squared error, while 3-room and 4-room flats give 66\%. Rows within 5~km of Raffles Place are 12\% of the rows and 16\% of the squared error. Jurong West gives the largest share of any town (9.6\%), and it also has the most holdout rows (Fig.~\ref{fig:town-errors} in Appendix~\ref{app:eda}).

\subsection{Importance and Partial Dependence}\label{sec:interpretation}

Fig.~\ref{fig:importance} shows permutation importance. Flat type ordinal (+166~SGD), subzone (+112~SGD) and floor area (+26~SGD) have the largest effects, and every other feature stays below 8~SGD. Month has the strongest numeric rank correlation with rent, but its effect here is only +0.06~SGD. The rent index carries the trend and is nearly flat over the holdout, so this result does not mean that time is unimportant. Related inputs can also share importance.

\centerline{\includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{permutation_importance}}
\caption{Permutation importance on 2{,}000 holdout rows. Bars are the mean rise in RMSE over five repeats, and error bars show their standard deviation.}
\label{fig:importance}

Fig.~\ref{fig:pdp} compares tuned LightGBM with a Ridge model on the same 39 inputs and the index target (RMSE 467.1, slightly different from the baseline Ridge in Table~\ref{tab:results}). Ridge has a straight response to MRT distance. LightGBM falls 46~SGD from 0.18 to 0.40~km and 33~SGD from there to 1.15~km. When lease year moves together with age, a flat 43 years older loses 210~SGD under LightGBM and 581~SGD under Ridge. LightGBM has lower error in every MRT distance band, and its gain varies by segment: Bukit Merah 3-room flats improve from 545 to 479~SGD, while Jurong West executive flats worsen from 580 to 601~SGD. These gains fit a nonlinear response and interactions, but they do not show which of the two caused the improvement.
