\section{Error Analysis and Interpretation}\label{sec:errors}

This section analyses the five model blend on the holdout, with tuned LightGBM on the index target as the comparison. The individual case review and the comparison with Ridge use tuned LightGBM only. Residual means prediction minus observed rent.

\subsection{Why the Error Is Not Lower}\label{sec:floor}

In this dataset, rows in the same cell and month have identical inputs, including the lag features, so a predictor using these inputs gives them one prediction. Even with the true mean of every group, the RMSE would stay at 178~SGD on all training rows, 165~SGD on the holdout and 187~SGD on the 16 month window. These are bounds for the observed groups, not forecast benchmarks: rows with unique inputs are assigned their true rent. Such rows make up 74\% of training rows and 75\% of holdout rows.

Rows that share inputs have a pooled within-group standard deviation of 449~SGD on the holdout and 468~SGD on all training rows. On 4{,}207 repeated holdout rows, the mean rent of the other rows in the group scores 612~SGD against 446~SGD for tuned LightGBM and 441~SGD for the blend. The group mean uses other holdout labels, so this is a descriptive comparison. The spread for unique profiles and the remaining achievable improvement are unknown; these figures do not establish a minimum RMSE for future rents.

\subsection{Residuals}\label{sec:residuals}

A few large errors carry much of the squared error. For the blend, absolute errors above SGD 1{,}000 occur in 3.3\% of holdout rows and give 34\% of the squared error, against 3.9\% and 38\% for tuned LightGBM (Fig.~\ref{fig:outliers}). The blend's 585 rows with a rent below SGD 2{,}000 are 3.4\% of the rows and give 24\% of the squared error, with a bias of $+1{,}011$~SGD. Of its 20 largest errors, 19 are over-predictions, and eight have a rent below SGD 1{,}000. In two pairs of rows the inputs are identical while the rents are SGD 3{,}550 and 800, and SGD 2{,}880 and 700.

The data cannot explain the very low rents. They may be for a room or one tenant's share of the rent, while area and type describe the whole flat. They may also be discounted agreements or errors in the recorded amount.

We found a matching HDB public record for a 5-room flat at 112A Depot Road in March 2025 with rent SGD~650~\cite{hdb_rental_records}. We used this table only to check the record. HDB describes the amounts as agreed rents declared by owners and says it does not verify their accuracy~\cite{hdb_rental_description}. This confirms that the low amount was published. The table has no unit number or room rental indicator, so the match cannot explain the rent or establish a recording error.

Tuned LightGBM also misses some high rents. Its ten largest under-predictions have observed rents of SGD~4{,}400 to 6{,}000 and give 0.95\% of its squared error. These include adjoined flats and a terrace flat model with only 14 fit rows. Their recorded areas are positive and their lease flags are valid. Sparse examples and missing unit details may limit prediction, but the review establishes no data error in these cases.

Large flats and central areas do not carry most of the blend's error. The 5-room and executive flats are 28\% of the rows and 31\% of the squared error, while 3-room and 4-room flats give 66\%. Rows within 5~km of Raffles Place are 12\% of the rows and 16\% of the squared error. Jurong West gives the largest share of any town (9.4\% for the blend, 9.6\% for tuned LightGBM), and it also has the most holdout rows (Fig.~\ref{fig:town-errors} in Appendix~\ref{app:eda}).

The blend gains most on rows with unique inputs, where the RMSE falls from 445.8 to 427.8~SGD. On rows that share inputs it falls from 445.8 to 440.6~SGD. Lag features can move the prediction for a whole group, but they cannot separate rows inside it.

Like the single models in Fig.~\ref{fig:monthly}, the blend shows no steady increase in error across the six holdout months, and it stays between 421 and 445~SGD per month. Its bias reaches $-67$~SGD in the last month, against a range of $-36$ to $-53$~SGD before it. Predicted rent deciles have a bias of $-74$ to $-18$~SGD, so the blend runs slightly low on average.

\subsection{Importance and Partial Dependence}\label{sec:interpretation}

Fig.~\ref{fig:importance} shows permutation importance for the blend. Flat type ordinal (+44~SGD) and the 24 month rent lags (+43~SGD) have the largest effects, ahead of flat type (+17~SGD), subzone (+13~SGD) and latitude (+12~SGD). The 12 month lags add +5~SGD and the 36 month lags +0.4~SGD, so the signal sits mostly at two years. Tuned LightGBM alone puts more weight on fewer inputs: flat type ordinal +166~SGD, subzone +112~SGD and floor area +26~SGD. Related inputs share importance, and the blend spreads it further across its members. Month has the strongest numeric rank correlation with rent, but its effect here is only +0.1~SGD, because the rent index carries the trend and is nearly flat over the holdout.

Fig.~\ref{fig:pdp} compares tuned LightGBM with matched Ridge ($\alpha = 1$) on the same 39 inputs and the index target (RMSE 467.1). The baseline Ridge in Table~\ref{tab:results} uses $\alpha = 10$ and 34 inputs. Matched Ridge has a straight response to MRT distance. The LightGBM prediction falls 46~SGD from 0.18 to 0.40~km and 33~SGD from there to 1.15~km. When age rises by 43 years and lease year moves with it, predicted rent falls by 210~SGD under LightGBM and 581~SGD under matched Ridge. These curves describe fitted predictions; they do not establish causal effects on rent. LightGBM has lower error in every MRT distance band, and its gain varies by segment: Bukit Merah 3-room flats improve from 545 to 479~SGD, while Jurong West executive flats worsen from 580 to 601~SGD. These gains fit a nonlinear response and interactions.
